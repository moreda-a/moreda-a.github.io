About

I am Mohammadreza Daneshvaramoli, a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Massachusetts Amherst since Spring 2023, with B.Sc. and M.Sc. degrees in Computer Engineering from Sharif University of Technology. My background combines theoretical computer science, algorithm design, mathematical optimization, and machine learning.

My research centers on learning-augmented online algorithms, exploring how machine-learned predictions can improve algorithmic performance without sacrificing worst-case guarantees. I study competitive analysis, consistency–robustness trade-offs, and decision-making under uncertainty, addressing problems in online selection, caching, scheduling, resource allocation, and the k-server problem.

I am interested in applying rigorous mathematical and computational methods to real-world challenges, particularly in quantitative research, large-scale optimization, and data-driven decision systems.

  4. Particle Aggregation Simulator (8086 Assembly Game): Built an interactive graphics simulation in 16-bit x86 (8086) assembly with about 1,600 lines of documented code. Moving particles freeze and stick when they touch the growing structure, and mode 2 grows a branching fractal pattern. Implemented pixel graphics with BIOS INT 10h, mouse control of the target with INT 33h, three simulation modes, a random number generator, and integer square root math for velocities. (Link to Github)
  5. RISC CPU Design: Designed a RISC processor in Verilog and VHDL using Intel/Altera Quartus II for an Altera FPGA. Built a 32-bit instruction register over a 16-bit datapath, a 32×16-bit register file, and an 8-operation ALU with add, subtract, left/right shifts, NAND, compare and max, plus overflow, zero, sign and equal flags. Also built a 16×16 multiplier, the PC, SP and address registers, a 256-word memory and control logic, and verified the design with waveform simulation. (Link to Github)

Research Projects

PhD Research Projects, University of Massachusetts Amherst, 2024–2026

  • On Worst-Case Guarantees for Graph-Based Vector Search. Accepted to NeurIPS 2026.
    • Proved worst-case lower bounds for graph-based nearest-neighbor search and developed constructions using Steiner points that achieve provably sublinear query time by simulating locality-sensitive hashing.
  • Fairness in the k-Server Problem. ITCS 2026.
    • The goal is to ensure no server bears excessive load while serving incoming requests (fairness notion) in a metric space.
    • Proved that there is no deterministic online algorithm that achieves a competitive ratio less than the diameter of the metric space while also being fair. Also, showed that for any non-constant k in a tree graph, the Double Coverage algorithm is not fair.
  • The Secretary Problem with Predictions and Chosen Order. ITCS 2026.
    • Proposed a new hiring problem where you can use machine-learned predictions and even choose interview order, with an algorithm that stays strong even if predictions are wrong.
  • Near-Optimal Consistency-Robustness Trade-Offs for Learning-Augmented Online Knapsack Problems. ICML 2025.
    • Developed learning-augmented online knapsack algorithms utilizing minimal predictive input—specifically, a single value or interval estimating the minimum value of items accepted by an optimal offline solution—achieving consistency and robustness.
  • Navigable Graphs for High-Dimensional Nearest Neighbor Search. Ongoing research.
    • Developing scalable navigable graph structures for high-dimensional nearest neighbor search, with a focus on establishing rigorous theoretical performance bounds for graph-based retrieval methods.
  • Calibrated Predictions for Learning-Augmented Caching. Ongoing research.
    • Designing online caching algorithms that leverage calibrated probabilistic machine-learned predictions for page reuse, and proving competitive guarantees that depend on calibration error.
    • The work introduces a binary prediction framework for caching and develops phase-based analyses connecting miscalibration to additional cache faults.
